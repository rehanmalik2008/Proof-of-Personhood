## Crossover (raw constraint count)

The IMT is cheaper than the list once **R > (added constraints at that depth)**:

| IMT depth | crossover R |
|---:|---:|
| 16 | ~7,196 |
| 20 | ~8,168 |
| 24 | ~9,140 |

Below the crossover the brute-force list wins on raw R1CS size; above it the IMT is not merely cheaper but the only option that stays bounded. The list also adds **R public inputs** (the verifier ships R field elements every verification); the IMT adds **one** (`revRoot`), so by verifier bandwidth and by proof/verification key size the IMT dominates well before the raw-constraint crossover.

## Recommended deployment

IMT **depth 20** (capacity ~1.05M revocations): total **12,477** constraints, constant in R, constant nPublic. This replaces the rev64/rev256/rev1024 circuit family with a single circuit whose cost does not grow with the revocation list.
